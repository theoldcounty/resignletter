Absolutely — this fits nicely as a specific promo format inside the larger catalog. I’d turn what you’ve written into a reusable Replit prompt called something like P21 — Show, Don’t Sell.

P21 — SCREEN-RECORDING SHORTS

“SHOW, DON’T SELL” ENGINE

Create a short-form promotional video for the application using a “show, don’t sell” philosophy.

The viewer should see the problem, watch the product being used, and see the real result.

Do not rely on talking heads or generic promotional claims.

The application itself should be the star.

⸻

ASSETS TO PREPARE

Use these supplied assets where available:

* 60-character tagline
* 150-character product description
* 512 × 512 logo
* 3 application screenshots
* Application URL / working application
* Relevant sample input
* Relevant sample output

Inspect the application and infer the most compelling legitimate use case.

Do not invent capabilities or claims.

⸻

VIDEO LENGTH

Target:

20–45 seconds

Optimise primarily for:

* TikTok
* Instagram Reels
* YouTube Shorts
* LinkedIn video
* X / social feeds

Use a vertical 9:16 composition by default.

⸻

SCENE 01 — THE HOOK

0–3 seconds

Start with the PROBLEM.

Do not begin with the logo.

Do not begin with a long title card.

Show something recognisably frustrating, messy, confusing, boring or time-consuming.

Possible examples:

* messy receipt
* PDF full of information
* spreadsheet
* blank form
* awkward email
* confusing document
* repetitive task
* multiple browser tabs
* raw data
* unstructured information

Use large, instantly readable text.

Example:

“STOP DOING THIS MANUALLY.”

or:

“Still doing [TASK] by hand?”

or:

“Got 47 pages to work through?”

Only use a specific claim if it accurately describes the demonstrated problem.

Use a rapid visual hook.

⸻

SCENE 02 — THE PROCESS

3–15 seconds

Show the actual application.

Use screen recording or a simulated recording based on the real interface.

Show:

1. Opening the application.
2. Selecting/uploading the relevant input.
3. The key interaction.
4. Payment if payment is genuinely part of the product.
5. Processing.
6. The transition toward the result.

Do not show unnecessary navigation.

Crop tightly around the important UI.

Use:

* cursor highlights
* click indicators
* zooms
* speed ramps
* subtle screen shake
* UI callouts
* accelerated playback

If the real process takes a long time, compress it visually while preserving the genuine sequence.

⸻

SCENE 03 — THE PAYOFF

15–30 seconds

This is the most important section.

Show the actual result.

Examples:

INPUT
→
ANALYSIS
→
PDF

or:

MESSY INFORMATION
→
STRUCTURED INFORMATION

or:

DOCUMENT
→
USEFUL OUTPUT

Make the result visually satisfying.

Zoom into the important part of the output.

Show the downloadable file, generated report, chart, recommendation, analysis or other actual result.

Use a short benefit statement.

Examples:

“Your [RESULT].”

“Ready to use.”

“From [INPUT] to [OUTPUT].”

If a genuine price point is part of the product, show it clearly.

For example:

“Get your [RESULT] for £1.”

Do not invent time-saving claims such as “in 10 seconds” unless the actual product reliably achieves that.

⸻

SCENE 04 — THE CTA

30–45 seconds

Finish with a simple action.

Examples:

“Try it.”

“Upload your first document.”

“Get your report.”

“See what it can do.”

“Try [PRODUCT] for £1.”

“Turn your [INPUT] into [OUTPUT].”

Then display:

* logo
* tagline
* URL
* relevant CTA button
* product interface

Keep the final frame on screen long enough to read.

⸻

VISUAL STYLE

Prioritise authenticity.

The video should feel like:

“Someone just showed me something genuinely useful.”

rather than:

“A company is trying to sell me something.”

Use real application UI whenever possible.

Use motion graphics to enhance the screen recording rather than conceal it.

Recommended visual techniques:

* punch-in zooms
* cursor tracking
* click highlights
* kinetic typography
* speed ramps
* subtle parallax
* animated arrows
* UI magnification
* progress animations
* before/after wipes
* document transformations
* clean transitions

⸻

AUDIO

Use energetic but unobtrusive music.

The music should support rapid pacing.

Add authentic UI sounds:

* click
* upload
* confirmation
* notification
* download
* subtle impact

Synchronise important actions with the soundtrack.

Optional:

Use a short voiceover, but only if it improves comprehension.

The video must work without sound.

⸻

TEXT RULE

Assume the viewer may watch with the sound OFF.

All critical information must therefore be understandable through:

* visuals
* captions
* interface
* short on-screen text

Avoid paragraphs.

Use short phrases.

Examples:

“MESSY INPUT”

“UPLOAD”

“ANALYSE”

“RESULT”

“DOWNLOAD”

“£1”

“TRY IT”

⸻

SCREEN-RECORDING RULES

Never spend more than a few seconds showing an unimportant interface.

If the user has to:

click A
→
click B
→
click C
→
click D

compress the sequence.

Highlight only the meaningful interaction.

The viewer should understand the workflow almost immediately.

⸻

MARKETING PRINCIPLE

Do not tell the viewer:

“Our revolutionary platform uses advanced technology to transform your workflow.”

Instead:

SHOW THE FILE.

SHOW THE UPLOAD.

SHOW THE BUTTON.

SHOW THE PROCESS.

SHOW THE RESULT.

SHOW THE VALUE.

⸻

OUTPUT

Produce:

1. 45-second master version.
2. 30-second condensed version.
3. 15-second social cut.

For each version provide:

* scene timings
* visual direction
* screen-recording actions
* on-screen text
* optional voiceover
* music direction
* transition direction
* CTA

The 15-second version should preserve the essential:

PROBLEM → PRODUCT → RESULT → CTA

sequence.

I’d also add three variants to your catalog

P21A — Raw Demo
Almost no motion graphics. Just the real screen recording, captions and satisfying UI sounds. This feels authentic.

P21B — Cinematic Demo
Same real workflow, but with zooms, parallax, animated typography, sound design and dramatic transitions.

P21C — Comedy Demo
Exaggerate the problem rather than exaggerating the product. For example, 37 browser tabs, papers flying everywhere, someone frantically searching through a PDF — then cut to the app calmly solving it.

That gives you a useful distinction:

Raw Demo = credibility
Cinematic Demo = polish
Comedy Demo = reach

And I’d make P21 the default social-video generator in your overall prompt library, while P01/P12/P18 are your more elaborate “campaign” treatments.
